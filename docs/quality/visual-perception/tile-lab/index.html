Tile Perception Lab

Experiments on how a fine pixel grid and a coarse grid of tiles, such as character cells, are seen. Each corner varies one choice and keeps the others fixed. All processing runs in this page; a file you open is not uploaded.

How to read the panels

Blind comparison. A viewer of a terminal image never sees the original. The blind button of a row hides the reference and the labels and shuffles the remaining panels; judge which looks most natural, then reveal.

Metrics. ΔE tile is the mean CIELAB ΔE between each output tile and the original tile. ΔE 3×3 compares averages over 3 × 3 tiles, the error that dithering reduces. Jumps is the share of neighboring tile pairs whose difference exceeds the original's by more than 6 ΔE: changes a viewer sees as structure that the picture does not contain. The marking option outlines them. The original tile colors are averaged in linear light, which is how light mixes physically. This choice of reference can favor linear-light mixing, and the metrics do not model the visibility of texture, so use them as hints and judge by eye.

Viewing geometry. Cycles per degree assume the density and distance entered above and one physical pixel per device pixel. Relative sensitivities use the luminance model of Mannos and Sakrison and the schematic chroma curve of the visual perception chapter.

Processing. Nearest palette colors are chosen with the selected color difference; averages and dithering errors are formed in the selected space; ordered and blue-noise offsets are scaled to the mean spacing of the palette levels in that space. DIN99d follows the formulas also used by chafa. Photographs are images/snake.jpg and images/egret.jpg from the libsixel repository.
